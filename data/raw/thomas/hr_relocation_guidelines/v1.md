## 1. Purpose

These guidelines tell People Operations how to set pay when an employee moves between operating
states.

## 2. Location-based pay bands

Each state belongs to a pay band. Salary ranges are set as a percentage of the Band A range.

| Band | States | Percentage of Band A |
|---|---|---|
| A | New York, Washington | 100% |
| B | Colorado, Illinois | 92% |
| C | Texas, Arizona | 85% |

## 3. Moves between bands

- **Moving to a lower band:** base pay is reduced to the same position in the new band's range,
  effective from the first full pay period after the move. Reductions are capped at **10%** of
  current base pay per move.
- **Moving to a higher band:** base pay is increased only at the next annual compensation review,
  not at the time of the move.
- Moves within the same band do not change pay.

Example: an engineer earning $150,000 in Band A who moves to Band C would calculate to
$127,500 (85%), but the 10% cap limits the reduction, so the new base pay is $135,000.

## 4. Payroll and tax setup

- Register state payroll tax for the new state before the first pay date after the move.
- Confirm the new address and work location in the HR system.

## 5. Relocation assistance

Thomas Software does not pay relocation assistance; all roles are remote and moves are employee
choices.

## 6. Records

Store band calculations in the employee's HR file. Do not share band percentages with managers
or employees; communicate only the employee's own new base pay.
